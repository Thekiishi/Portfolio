Programme Overview

PAID – Roots and Roofs was a cultural preservation and skills development initiative funded by the European Union's Spaces of Culture programme and implemented by the EUNIC Cluster Nigeria, a coalition of European cultural institutes and embassies operating in Nigeria. Goethe-Institut Nigeria served as the administrative and financial lead.

The Roots and Roofs pilot theme, African Inspired Interior and Exterior Architecture, sought to document, preserve, and revitalise indigenous Nigerian architectural heritage at risk of being lost to rapid urbanisation and generational disconnect. The programme selected young Nigerian creative professionals working in pairs (an architect/designer and a photographer/visual storyteller) from each of Nigeria's six geopolitical zones and the FCT.

Selected teams were trained by expert facilitators in indigenous architectural techniques, then deployed back to their communities to execute small-scale projects and produce documentary outputs. The selection process ran in two stages: Steering Committee panels evaluated applicants zone by zone to produce a longlist, which a five-member Final Jury reduced to the confirmed cohort. Training spanned virtual workshops (February 9–10, 2026) and onsite sessions in Lagos (from February 16, 2026).

My Role & Responsibilities

I joined the PAID programme as Programme Communications Coordinator from January 2026, with a formal contract from January 1, 2026. My remit spanned three interconnected workstreams.

Facilitator Coordination & Pipeline Management

The most operationally demanding aspect of my role. With approximately 15 confirmed facilitators (9 virtual, 6 physical) and a full roster reaching 15, each with unique session requirements and four documentation categories to track. I built and maintained the pipeline that enabled both workshop phases to launch on schedule.

  • Onboarded confirmed facilitators by specialism and zone, aligning each to specific workshop sessions
  • Sent individualised briefings confirming session topics, formats, timings, and expectations
  • Built and maintained a tracking system for outstanding submissions, invoices, contracts, addresses, and presentation materials
  • Chased outstanding items systematically, ensuring all facilitators were fully onboarded ahead of workshop dates
  • Coordinated logistics for February 9–10 virtual workshops and February 16 Lagos onsite sessions, both launched on schedule
Participant & Stakeholder Communications

Managing communications across 24 participants, 5 jury members, 12 Steering Committee representatives, and 4 partner organisation leads simultaneously required consistent tone and structured follow-up across a high volume of concurrent threads.

  • Coordinated participant onboarding across all six geopolitical zones: collected bios, profile photos, travel details, and project submissions
  • Liaised with the five-member Final Jury on selection timelines and documentation
  • Managed communications with Steering Committee members from all four partner institutions plus IADS, National Troupe, CBAAC, and the EU Delegation
  • Maintained and corrected programme timeline documentation
  • Drafted formal programme correspondence for all stakeholder groups under Goethe-Institut's institutional authority
Programme Communications & Social Media Strategy

I developed the social media strategy and content calendar for @paid_rootsandroofs across Instagram and Facebook, covering the programme lifecycle from open call through the workshop phase.

  • Designed a phased content strategy aligned to programme milestones, open call, partner spotlights, jury introductions, workshop coverage
  • Wrote all captions and carousel copy introducing partners, jury members, and the programme's cultural mission
  • Provided live coverage of the February 9–10 virtual workshops and Day 1 of the February 16 onsite Lagos sessions
  • Created accessible public-facing content translating a specialist cultural heritage mission for broader audiences

Key Contributions & Impact

Facilitator Coordination System

The facilitator pipeline was the backbone of programme delivery. Building a structured tracking system for 15 facilitators, each with session-specific requirements and four documentation categories, was the clearest demonstration of project management capability in my work. Both the February 9–10 virtual workshops and the February 16 Lagos onsite sessions launched on schedule with this infrastructure in place.

Multi-Partner Stakeholder Communications

Operating across four European cultural institutions, 12 committee members, a five-person jury, 24 participants, and 15 facilitators, each with different roles and communication needs, I maintained consistent, professionally calibrated correspondence throughout. All communications were drafted under Goethe-Institut's institutional authority, requiring particular care with tone, accuracy, and the representation of partner interests.

Social Media Infrastructure

I established the @paid_rootsandroofs social media presence from the ground up: strategy, content calendar, copy, and live workshop coverage. This created a public-facing record of the programme's cultural heritage mission extending its reach beyond the immediate participant and partner community.

Professional Handover

When the contract concluded on February 17, 2026, I completed a full structured handover covering social media accounts, all facilitator and participant contacts, programme timelines, and outstanding items, under significant time pressure, in the middle of live workshops. Continuity of programme delivery was maintained.

Reflections & Learnings

"The facilitator coordination workstream was the most operationally demanding I have managed to date, and the clearest evidence, to me, of what project management actually looks like when it matters."

The PAID programme gave me hands-on experience coordinating a genuinely complex stakeholder environment, operating across four European cultural institutions, multiple Nigerian government and civil society bodies, and a nationally distributed cohort of creative professionals. The multi-partner structure meant every communication carried institutional weight, requiring careful stakeholder management and precise documentation.

Building a structured pipeline for 15 facilitators, each with unique session requirements, documentation obligations, and timelines, while managing participant onboarding and live social media across two concurrent workstreams reinforced my core approach to delivery: visibility, proactive follow-up, and clear ownership of every outstanding item.

The contract's early conclusion during live workshops was an uncommon circumstance. My response, completing a full, structured handover document the same day, reflects my understanding that professional responsibility to a programme and its participants exists independently of contractual circumstances.

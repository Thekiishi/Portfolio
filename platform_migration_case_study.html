Project Overview

This project planning exercise was developed as a comprehensive PM portfolio piece, demonstrating full project documentation for a realistic enterprise migration scenario. The scenario: TechCorp has acquired a modern platform and must migrate 50 enterprise clients from a legacy system within 90 days, with zero tolerance for data loss and $450K in active client contracts at stake.

The exercise covers the complete documentation a project manager would produce at initiation and planning stage: stakeholder analysis, risk register, phased delivery plan, communication strategy, and budget framework. A cross-functional team of 15 across engineering, client success, QA, and content is assumed.

The projected outcomes modelled in the plan: delivery in 11 weeks against a 12-week schedule, 8% under the $250K budget, 92% client satisfaction, and zero critical incidents, based on the risk mitigations and phased approach designed.

02

The Challenge

The scenario was designed around four intersecting constraints that a real PM would need to navigate simultaneously.

  • Client diversity: 50 enterprise clients with widely varying technical capabilities, content volumes, and integration requirements. A one-size approach would not work.
  • SEO preservation: Each client had organic search rankings built over years. Any broken redirects or missed metadata would immediately damage their visibility and trust in TechCorp.
  • Zero downtime requirement: Clients could not experience service interruptions. Migrations had to be scheduled, tested, and cut over without gaps in live service.
  • Compressed timeline: 90 days to migrate 50 clients while maintaining business as usual for the client success and engineering teams, who had other responsibilities running concurrently.
03

My Approach

Phased Rollout with Client Tiering

Rather than migrating clients in a single wave, I designed a three-phase rollout based on client complexity and risk level. Lower-complexity clients migrated first, providing real-world validation before tackling the highest-value accounts. This sequencing protected the most critical relationships and gave the team a learning curve without exposing TechCorp to maximum risk from day one.

Pre-Migration Audit and Documentation

Before any migration began, I led a full audit of all 50 client environments: content volumes, active integrations, redirect requirements, and custom configurations. This produced a per-client migration brief that the engineering team used as a single source of truth, eliminating guesswork and reducing back-and-forth during execution.

Structured Risk Register and Contingency Planning

I built a risk register covering technical, timeline, and client-facing risks, each with an assigned owner, likelihood rating, and mitigation plan. A 15% budget contingency was built in from the start. Weekly risk reviews kept the register live throughout the project rather than treating it as a one-time document.

Client Communication Cadence

Each client received advance notice of their migration window, a plain-language explanation of what to expect, and a named point of contact from the client success team. Post-migration, a structured follow-up was sent within 48 hours to confirm satisfaction and capture any outstanding issues before they escalated.

Risk Management

The risk register tracked six primary risks throughout the project lifecycle.

Projected Outcomes

Planned for Early Delivery and Budget Efficiency

The project plan models delivery in 11 weeks against a 12-week schedule, with a 15% contingency reserve built in from the start. The phased rollout design and pre-migration audit were specifically structured to protect the timeline against the most likely sources of delay.

Risk Mitigations Designed for Zero Incidents

The risk register and contingency planning were built around the goal of zero client-facing incidents. Every high-rated risk has a documented mitigation, a rollback procedure, and an assigned owner, so the response is pre-decided rather than reactive when issues arise.

Communication Strategy Targeting 92% Satisfaction

The client communication plan was designed with a 92% satisfaction target. Each client receives advance notice, a named contact, a plain-language migration guide, and a 48-hour post-migration follow-up. The strategy addresses the specific drivers of satisfaction identified in similar enterprise migrations.

$450K Revenue Risk Identified and Mitigated

The planning documentation identifies $450K in active client contracts as the primary business risk driver. Contract penalty clauses informed the 90-day hard deadline, and the phased rollout was sequenced specifically to protect the highest-value accounts from early-phase execution risk.

08

Reflections & Learnings

"The pre-migration audit is the single highest-leverage investment in a project like this. Every hour spent documenting client environments upfront saves multiples of that time during execution."

The decision to tier clients by complexity and migrate in phases rather than attempting a simultaneous rollout is the most important structural choice in this plan. It gives the team a learning curve before touching the highest-risk accounts, and means that by the time the most complex clients are reached, the majority of execution problems have already been encountered and solved on lower-stakes migrations.

The communication cadence is time-consuming to set up but directly drives satisfaction. In enterprise migrations, clients who feel informed and have a clear point of contact rarely escalate. Building follow-up into the process rather than making it reactive is the difference between a minor issue and a contract conversation.

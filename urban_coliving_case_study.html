Project Overview

This capstone project from the Google Project Management Certificate programme tasked me with designing a product from the ground up using the full PM toolkit: problem framing, user research, persona development, feature prioritisation, roadmap creation, and success metrics definition.

I chose to tackle affordable co-living for young professionals in urban centres, a problem shaped by rising rents, limited housing supply, and the particular challenges of trust and safety that make shared living unappealing even when it is financially necessary. The goal was to design a platform that made co-living genuinely accessible, transparent, and safe, not just cheaper.

The project ran across 8 weeks, following Agile sprint cycles, and culminated in a validated product strategy supported by primary user research with 15 participants.

02

The Problem

Young professionals in major cities face a housing affordability gap that is too large to bridge through individual renting but too uncomfortable to solve through traditional shared housing. The existing options carry specific, recurring friction points that my research surfaced consistently.

  • Trust and safety: Moving in with strangers without a reliable vetting or verification process is a barrier many potential users cannot get past, particularly for women living alone.
  • Transparency: Hidden costs, ambiguous house rules, and undisclosed property conditions create disputes after move-in that erode trust in shared platforms.
  • Compatibility matching: Random or arbitrary roommate pairing leads to lifestyle mismatches, which produce conflict and high turnover, making co-living feel unstable rather than affordable.
  • Lack of community: Purely transactional housing arrangements do not address the social isolation that young professionals in new cities frequently experience.
03

User Research

I conducted 15 in-depth interviews with young professionals aged 22 to 34, targeting people currently renting alone, currently in shared housing, or actively searching for affordable options. Interviews averaged 45 minutes and followed a semi-structured format that allowed participants to take the conversation where their experience led.

Key Research Findings
  • 87% of participants cited safety and trust as their primary barrier to co-living, ahead of cost and availability
  • 73% said they would adopt a co-living platform if it included verified profiles, transparent pricing, and a compatibility matching feature
  • 68% had previously tried a shared housing arrangement that ended due to lifestyle incompatibility rather than financial reasons
  • Community features were valued by 61% of participants, but only as a secondary consideration after safety and transparency were addressed
  • Women participants (9 of 15) placed significantly higher emphasis on safety verification, with several describing past experiences that made unverified platforms feel unusable

"The idea is right, the matching is just always wrong."
05

Product Strategy

Research findings shaped a product strategy organised around three core principles: verify first, match meaningfully, and price transparently. Feature prioritisation used a value vs. effort matrix, with safety and verification features classified as non-negotiable foundations regardless of build cost.

Priority Features (MVP)
  • Verified identity and background checks: Mandatory for all users before any listing or enquiry. Government ID verification plus optional professional verification.
  • Lifestyle compatibility questionnaire: Structured pre-match survey covering sleep schedules, cleanliness standards, social preferences, work patterns, and guest policies.
  • Transparent pricing breakdown: All-inclusive cost display from first interaction: rent, utilities, service fees, and any optional add-ons clearly itemised before any commitment.
  • Secure in-platform messaging: All pre-move communication on-platform, with message history preserved. No exchange of personal contact details until both parties confirm interest.
Phase 2 Features (Post-MVP)
  • Community events calendar and shared interest groups per property
  • In-app maintenance request and issue tracking
  • Review system for both hosts and co-livers post-stay
  • Flexible short-term lease options (3 to 6 months)

08

Reflections & Learnings

"The research reframed everything. What I thought was a pricing problem turned out to be a trust problem. That single shift changed the entire product strategy."

Going into the research phase, I expected affordability to dominate the findings. It came third. Safety and trust were the real barriers, and most participants had mentally accepted the cost of co-living long before they encountered the platform trust problem. That finding reversed the priority order for nearly every feature decision that followed.

The discipline of defining success metrics before building the roadmap was the most practically useful part of the process. It forced clarity on what "working" actually meant for this product, and surfaced the fact that traditional engagement metrics like active users and session length would tell you almost nothing about whether the core promise was being kept.

If I were taking this to a real product team, the next step would be a prototype usability test focused specifically on the verification and matching flows, where the gap between what users say they want and what they will actually complete in practice is likely to be widest.
